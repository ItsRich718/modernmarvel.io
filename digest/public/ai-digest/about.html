About

A weekly design practice with a public forcing function

Modern Marvel publishes one edition a week. Each edition applies a different design directive to the same system and the same kind of data, so the archive becomes a gallery of design arguments rather than a run of back issues. The analysis is real and the method is published, because a dashboard that hides how its data was gathered is a worse portfolio piece, not a better one.

Who this is for

Written for an AI-curious reader, not only for practitioners

Operators in financial services and people building with AI are the core readership, but every page is written to be legible to an educated reader outside the field. Where the method uses a term of art, the term is defined on the page rather than assumed.

The method

Agreement between independent sources is the product, not coverage volume

A fixed set of newsletters is read every weekday. Each item is reduced to a claim of twenty words or fewer and tagged against a fixed vocabulary of ten themes. Nothing is summarized newsletter by newsletter, because that measures how much was published rather than what is true.

Each source carries an independence class. An aggregator reports the day's news and works the same press cycle as every other aggregator. A primary analysis title makes its own argument and would still have something to say in a quiet week. Three aggregators reporting the same launch is one press release counted three times.

Before anything is counted, three collapses are applied:

  • One sighting per publisher per day. A title that runs six items on one subject in one issue has said one thing.
  • One count per publisher. Newsletter operators increasingly run several titles. Without this rule, a house view repeated across four of them scores as four-way agreement, which is a publishing strategy detecting itself.
  • Sponsored placements carry volume, never independence. A paid listing is bought, not reasoned. It appears in the volume figure and is removed before publishers and classes are counted.

Inside a rolling seven days, a theme is converging when at least three collapsed sightings, at least three publishers and at least two independence classes carry it. It is contested when sources carry it with opposing valence, which is the most interesting state the system produces and is published rather than resolved. Everything else is an echo: real coverage volume, reported with its count and never with a conclusion.

The system needs at least three active sources spanning at least two independence classes for convergence to be possible at all. That floor is checked and stated every week, because "nothing converged" and "nothing could converge" are different statements and only one of them is about the AI industry.

Known bias

Two of the three independent voices are finance publications

The independence base is deliberately small and it is not neutral. Two of the three primary analysis titles cover corporate finance and accounting, so themes that touch disclosure, cost structure and workforce composition have an easier path to convergence than themes that do not. A theme in, say, consumer product or robotics can be real and still never clear the bar here, simply because no independent title in this set covers that beat.

The other structural limit is timing. All three independent titles publish weekly, on three different weekdays, so every converging theme expires on its own author's next publication day. Each edition states which issues are holding the week's findings up.

Sources are named by title. Coverage is drawn from a personal newsletter subscription list, not from any employer, system or colleague.

The design premise

The variation is the show, and the shell never varies

Each edition applies one directive from a fixed rotation: typographic hierarchy, data-density inversion, single-column narrative, chart-forward, editorial, monochrome discipline, progressive disclosure, comparison-first. A directive may not repeat inside 28 days. The applied variation is named at the top of every edition, because unlabeled change reads as inconsistency while labeled change reads as intent.

The directive governs layout and emphasis only. The masthead, navigation, archive, this page, the designer credit and the footer are a constant shell. Without a constant shell there is no publication for the variation to vary against, only a series of unrelated pages. Changing the color system is not a variation; it is abandoning the design system.

The accessibility floor holds regardless of the directive: 4.5 to 1 contrast, visible focus rings, full keyboard operability, 44 pixel touch targets, and status never carried by color alone. Every edition is a single self-contained file with no scripts, no external fonts, no images, no analytics and no tracking. It renders identically offline.

When a week's design cannot be done properly, the edition ships in a reduced format, labeled as such, rather than not shipping. A gap in the archive damages the premise more than a plain edition does.

Every edition publishes its own critique

Three questions, answered on the page

What was the design decision this week, and did the data support it or fight it? What would a designer criticize first? Which section earned its space least? A gallery without critique is a portfolio. A gallery with critique is a practice.

Editions are immutable once published. A past edition is never retro-edited to fix a design since outgrown, because that destroys the record the archive exists to keep.

How this edition was assembled

Seven newsletters reported inside the rolling seven-day window to September 1, 2026: four aggregators (TLDR AI, GenAI Works, The Rundown AI, Superhuman AI) and three primary analysis titles (Import AI, Cook's Playbooks, The CFO Desk). One aggregator title on the source list has published nothing since August 1 and contributed nothing.

  • Convergence floor: met. Seven publishers and both independence classes reported inside the window.
  • Independent evidence: only two publishers supplied any. The CFO Desk published on cadence and contributed none, because its single tagged item was a paid placement and the sponsored rule removes those before independence is counted.
  • Volume: 216 raw sightings. Every figure on this page is reported after collapse, never as a raw count alone.
  • Sponsored: 32 of 216 sightings, 14.8 percent, were paid placements. They count toward volume and never toward publisher or independence counts.
  • Known gap: a scheduled capture did not run on August 31 and was recovered by hand on September 1. No edition was lost, but the record shows the gap.
