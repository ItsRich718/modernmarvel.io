AI Digest Engine

Compounds daily

A scheduled pipeline that reads every AI newsletter landing in my inbox, extracts themes and tools into a persistent ledger, and emails back a designed brief each morning. On Fridays it synthesizes the week and publishes a public edition.

Most people consume AI news and forget it. This accumulates it. The ledger knows what it saw last month, so it can tell me what's actually new.

Background

Where the day job overlaps with the tools.

Sixteen years turning regulation and operating-model change into things that actually ship, the last decade of it in financial services. Today that means retirement: a SECURE 2.0 implementation program, wealth-management and operating-model strategy, and the unglamorous execution work that decides whether a strategy ever reaches a participant. Before retirement it was the enterprise LIBOR transition; before that, standing up program governance and post-merger integration for a de-novo bank.

The programs are matrixed, the delivery is part Agile and part waterfall because that is what large regulated firms actually run, and most of the work sits in the gap between a decision and the thing shipping, which is where operating models either hold or quietly fail.

What I've come to care about is leverage. Not tools for their own sake: the narrower question of what it takes to make expertise repeatable, so that solving a problem once means having solved a category of problems. Inside a regulated firm that question has a second half: whether the method can be evidenced afterwards. Anything that can't be is a demo.

So I build. The three above started as my own bottlenecks. I was reading more than I could retain, rebuilding the same analysis from scratch, watching a good program die inside a spreadsheet. Each is a test of the same idea: the durable asset is the method, not the output. One runs on a schedule and keeps a ledger. One is a library I call rather than rewrite. One runs entirely offline, because the constraint was a gym with no signal. All three are built to be audited rather than admired.

An MBA and a BA in economics, both from SUNY Albany, and a senior leaders program at Wharton's Aresty Institute. Away from the job I'm a residential landlord and spent four years as treasurer of an HOA board, the same thesis in slower motion, with real money and no undo button.
